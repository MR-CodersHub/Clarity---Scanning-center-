Preparation Checklist

How to prepare for your CT scan

Adhering to these simple guidelines ensures crisp imaging and smooth workflow:

1
Fasting for Contrast Studies (CECT)

If your scan requires intravenous contrast media, refrain from eating solid food 4 hours prior. Clear water is encouraged.

2
Recent Kidney Function Blood Test

Patients receiving contrast must provide a serum creatinine report taken within the past 30 days to ensure renal safety.

3
Oral Hydration for Abdominal Scans

Abdominal CT examinations may involve drinking a dilute oral contrast beverage over 45 minutes to delineate bowel loops.

Frequently Asked CT Questions

Answers to common questions regarding CT radiation safety, contrast administration, and preparation.

Our 128-slice scanner uses advanced model-based iterative reconstruction (ASiR-V) which dramatically lowers the radiation dose by up to 75% compared to legacy scanners, delivering the diagnostic equivalent of natural background environmental radiation over several months.
When intravenous non-ionic contrast is administered, you may feel a warm sensation spreading throughout your body, a brief metallic taste in your mouth, or the sensation of needing to urinate. This is completely harmless and subsides in under 60 seconds.
Intravenous contrast media is eliminated naturally through the kidneys. A recent serum creatinine blood test verifies normal renal function before administration to safeguard your kidney health. Non-contrast scans do not require this test.
A CT scan uses low-dose X-rays and takes only a few seconds to minutes, making it outstanding for bones, lungs, acute trauma, and vascular imaging. An MRI uses magnetic fields with zero radiation and takes 20-30 minutes, specializing in soft tissues like the brain, spinal cord, and cartilage.
Elective CT scans are generally avoided during pregnancy to prevent radiation exposure to the developing fetus. In most cases, radiation-free alternatives such as Ultrasound or MRI are recommended after clinical consultation.
